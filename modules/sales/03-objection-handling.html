The Anatomy of a Strong Response

Objections are not resistance — they are engagement. A prospect who objects is thinking about your proposal; a prospect who goes quiet has already decided. Your job is to treat every objection as information about the real concern, then answer that concern with evidence.

Listen to the full objection without interrupting, acknowledge it genuinely, and ask one clarifying question before responding. Most objections are shorthand for something deeper: “too expensive” usually means “I can’t yet justify this to my budget holder,” and “we already have a tool” usually means “prove the switching cost is worth it.”

💡 Acknowledge → Reframe → Prove → Advance

Every strong response does four things: acknowledge the concern as legitimate, reframe it around the underlying goal, prove your answer with customer evidence, and advance with a concrete next step — usually a demo or a proof of value on their code.

The Six Objections You Will Hear Most

“We already use Snyk / Mend / GitHub Advanced Security.”

Acknowledge: “That’s a solid tool and it clearly works for you.” Reframe: the question isn’t whether it finds vulnerabilities — it’s how much of the backlog is noise, how fast findings get fixed, and who governs the AI coding tools now writing code. Prove and advance: offer to run reachability side by side on two or three of their repositories so they can see how much of their critical backlog they never needed to work on. Keep it classy — never attack the incumbent.

“It’s too expensive.”

Acknowledge: “Budget scrutiny is fair — every tool should pay for itself.” Reframe: the expensive thing is the status quo. Quantify the triage tax: how many engineer-hours a week does the team spend investigating findings that turn out to be unreachable? Egnyte cut mean time to remediation by 70% — that is engineering capacity, not license cost. Advance: size the right subscription together (developers, languages, repos, scan volume) and route the quote through the partner portal so the numbers are real.

“Our developers won’t adopt another tool.”

Acknowledge: “If developers reject it, the deployment fails — you’re right to care.” Reframe: Endor Labs is developer-first: the MCP server runs inside Cursor, Claude Code, Codex, VS Code, Gemini CLI, Antigravity, and IntelliJ; fixes arrive as pull requests; CI/CD integrations use keyless OIDC; and the developer tier for AURI and MCP tools is free. Astronomer’s CISO called the effect “noise canceling headphones for vulnerability management” — developers adopt a tool that removes work.

“Reachability sounds too good to be true.”

Acknowledge: “A 97% noise reduction sounds like marketing — I’d be skeptical too.” Reframe: it’s engineering, not magic. Endor Labs resolves the dependency tree, builds the call graph from application code through direct and transitive dependencies, and maps which vulnerable functions are actually called — in most applications only a small fraction of reported CVEs is reachable. Prove and advance: Zebra Technologies saw 97% of SCA noise disappear, and a POV on their own repos will show the same mechanics live.

“We don’t have AI coding yet.”

Acknowledge: “Maybe not as a formal program.” Reframe: developers adopt coding agents and MCP servers on their own, so AURI usually finds more running than anyone expected — inventory comes first, control comes before scale. Getting governance in place before you need it is the whole point; retrofitting after an incident is far harder. Advance: the developer tier for AURI and MCP tools is free, so there is no budget barrier to starting now.

“Endor Labs SAST is new — we already have a scanner.”

Acknowledge: “Your current scanner earned its place.” Reframe: Endor Labs SAST pairs rule-based scanning with AI SAST agents that review every pull request for the architectural risks rule-based scanners miss — with up to 95% fewer false positives and 2.6x more true positives than frontier AI models alone. One system from finding to fix also means SCA and SAST findings share one triage, one policy engine, one backlog. Advance: include a SAST pass in the POV and let the results argue for you.

Negotiation Fundamentals

Most application security negotiations are won before price is ever discussed — by anchoring the value of noise reduction and fast fixes, and by knowing the buyer’s approval path in advance.

  • Anchor value before price. The buyer should already know what a week of triage costs them before your number arrives.
  • Trade, don’t concede. Every concession earns a commitment: a longer term, a case study, a reference call, or a named expansion.
  • Multi-year structures. Where it helps the customer lock predictability, multi-year agreements are worth real terms — negotiate them deliberately, never reflexively.
  • Build the champion. Give your internal advocate the numbers and proof points they need to sell the deal when you are not in the room.
  • Know the paper process. Security review, legal, and procurement each add weeks; ask about them early, not after verbal approval.

⚠️ Never Quote Numbers You Cannot Stand Behind

Pricing and margin for registered deals come from the partner portal — not from memory or estimation. A single invented number can destroy trust, blow up the commercial conversation, and cost you the margin protection that deal registration provides. When a prospect pushes for figures on the spot, commit to a date instead of a discount.
