Demo Fundamentals: Prepare, Personalize, Prove

A great demo is not a product tour — it is proof that the prospect’s specific pain disappears inside their own workflow. That only happens when discovery drives the demo: you should be able to name their top three pains, their main languages, and their incumbent tool before you open the platform.

Prepare every demo with this checklist:

  • Confirm the pain. Open by restating what they told you in discovery — and confirm it is still the priority.
  • Know the stack. Languages, repos, CI/CD systems, and where the developers actually work (which IDEs and agents).
  • Pre-scan a representative repo. Never scan live for the first time in front of a customer; have the results ready.
  • Line up proof points. Zebra Technologies’ 97% noise cut and Egnyte’s 70% faster MTTR attach credibility to each capability you show.
  • Agree the agenda. Tell them what you will show, for how long, and what the next step will be.

💡 Show the Backlog Shrink, Not the Feature List

The moment that wins the room is not a dashboard — it is a critical-severity backlog visibly collapsing to the handful of findings that are actually reachable. Build the demo so that moment happens early.

The Standard Demo Flow

Master this five-step sequence. It tells one continuous story — find what matters, fix it fast, prevent what should never arrive, and review code like a security expert — and every step maps to a pillar from Module 01.

  1. Scan. Connect a repository through CI/CD or the endorctl CLI with keyless OIDC auth. Show the dependency tree resolving quickly across their languages — reachability covers 40+ of them.
  2. Reachability triage. Show function-level reachability classifying findings, then walk one call path from their code into the vulnerable function. Watch the backlog shrink — Zebra cut SCA noise by 97% this way.
  3. Remediation. Open a reachable finding and show AI-powered remediation and upgrade impact analysis, fixes arriving as automated pull requests. When no upstream fix exists, show Endor Patches backporting the fix for Java or Python. Tie it to Egnyte’s 70% faster MTTR.
  4. Package Firewall block. Attempt to install a typosquatted or malicious package and let the firewall block it at install time — before it reaches a developer machine or CI. Supply chain prevention, not just detection.
  5. AI SAST PR review. Open a pull request and let the AI SAST agent review it for the architectural risks rule-based scanners miss, with up to 95% fewer false positives and 2.6x more true positives than frontier AI models. For AI-forward audiences, close with AURI: agent inventory and allow/block/ask-a-human policies.

The full flow runs 20–30 minutes. Trim it to the room: a VP Engineering audience wants steps 2 and 3; a CISO evaluating AI governance wants AURI first. Never skip step 2 — reachability is the story.

Designing a Proof of Value

A POV turns your claims into the prospect’s own evidence, on their own code. Run it as a structured evaluation with a fixed window — typically two to four weeks — not an open-ended trial. The design work happens before day one.

  • Success criteria (agree in writing): noise reduction percentage (reachable vs. total findings), improvement in mean time to remediation on reachable findings, and coverage (share of the critical backlog classified with a reachability verdict).
  • Scope: three to five representative repositories across their key languages — enough to be meaningful, small enough to finish inside the window.
  • Timeline: week one, connect repos and baseline current state; weeks two and three, run triage and remediation workflows with the team; final week, measure results against the agreed criteria.
  • Stakeholders: a security lead as owner, an engineering manager for repo access, at least two developers exercising the workflow, and an executive sponsor for the final readout.

Define the criteria so the results write the business case for you: “X% of critical findings were unreachable noise” and “MTTR improved Y%” are numbers a champion can carry straight into a budget conversation.

POV Pitfalls and Follow-Through

POVs fail for predictable reasons. Sidestep them and your close rate climbs.

  • Scope creep. “Let’s add the other 400 repos” kills the timeline — hold to the agreed scope and add repositories after signature instead.
  • Vague criteria. “See if you like it” is not a success criterion; without written criteria, goalposts move at the readout.
  • No developers. A POV run only by security proves nothing about adoption — the people who fix code must be in the workflow.
  • No executive sponsor. Without a budget owner tracking the results, even a winning POV stalls in committee.
  • Drift past the window. Open-ended evaluations lose urgency; a fixed end date keeps the decision live.

Then follow through deliberately: hold a checkpoint every week, review the mid-point together, and end with a readout that presents before/after numbers against the agreed criteria — co-presented with the Endor Labs sales engineer on registered deals. Close the readout by proposing the commercial step: sizing the subscription through the partner portal (Module 06) with the deal already registered (Module 05).

⚠️ Never Start a POV Without Written Criteria

The single most common POV failure is starting before success is defined. If you cannot get the prospect to agree on measurable criteria and a named owner, you do not have a POV — you have a free trial and a distracted champion.
