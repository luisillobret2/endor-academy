The Competitive Landscape for Application Security

Application security is a crowded market, and most of your opportunities already have an incumbent: Snyk, Mend.io, Veracode, Checkmarx, Black Duck, GitHub Advanced Security, or Sonatype. You will rarely win by attacking the incumbent head-on. You win by reframing the evaluation around what the incumbent cannot do — and what Endor Labs does better than anyone.

That reframe has three parts. Reachability: function-level analysis across 40+ languages means the backlog is the vulnerabilities the code actually calls — Zebra Technologies cut SCA noise by 97%. Fix, not just find: AI-powered remediation and Endor Patches close findings, not just report them. Agentic security: Coding Agent Governance (AURI) and AI SAST cover the AI coding era, and no major competitor has an equivalent governance layer.

💡 The Reframe That Wins

“Every tool on your shortlist finds vulnerabilities. The question is which platform tells you which few actually matter in your code, fixes them, and governs the AI agents now writing your code. That’s the gap we fill.”

Each competitor has real strengths — acknowledge them, then contrast on outcomes. The table below summarizes your win themes; the full battlecards live in the academy’s Resource Library (vs. Snyk, Mend.io, Veracode, Checkmarx, Black Duck, GitHub Advanced Security, and Sonatype).

Displacement Plays: When to Engage

Timing decides most competitive deals. Even the perfect pitch fails against an incumbent with eleven months left on its contract, and a mediocre pitch wins when the renewal window is open. Watch for three triggers:

  • Renewal windows — engage 90–120 days before the incumbent renewal, when budgets are being re-examined and switching costs are at their lowest.
  • Noise complaints — triage burden, missed SLAs, or a security team that has stopped trusting its own findings are open invitations for reachability.
  • AI initiatives — when the prospect rolls out coding agents or MCP servers and asks “how do we govern this?”, no incumbent has a credible answer. Arrive with AURI.

When a trigger fires, run this displacement motion:

  1. Quantify the current pain — triage hours, backlog size, MTTR, and SLA misses under the incumbent.
  2. Show the reachability delta — scan two or three representative repos side by side and let the shrinking backlog make the argument.
  3. Time the renewal window — align the POV conclusion with the incumbent’s decision point so results arrive when budget is live.
  4. Co-sell with Endor Labs — bring in the channel manager and a sales engineer for competitive deals; register the opportunity first.
  5. Close on agreed criteria — define POV success criteria in writing so the decision is evidence, not opinion.

Using Battlecards the Right Way

The academy’s battlecards are built for the moments when a competitor comes up: they cover where each competitor genuinely wins, where Endor Labs wins, the proof points that land, and the traps to avoid. Read them before every competitive meeting — not during it.

Preparation beats improvisation. Before the meeting, know the incumbent’s contract end date, what the prospect actually likes about it, and the top complaint you heard in discovery. Then ask questions that surface the gaps the buyer can name themselves: “What portion of your critical findings turned out to be exploitable?” or “How does your current tool handle the coding agents your teams are running?”

And never take the bait when a prospect asks “why is competitor X bad?” Answer with outcomes and proof instead — that discipline is what separates trusted advisors from vendors.

⚠️ Keep It Classy

Contrast on outcomes, never disparage. Your prospect chose the incumbent, may like the incumbent’s team, and will remember how you talked about them. “Our customers cut SCA noise by 97%” wins arguments; “their scanner is garbage” loses them — and sometimes the deal.
