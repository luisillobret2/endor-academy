What Is Software Composition Analysis?

Modern applications are assembled, not written from scratch. The overwhelming majority of the code in a typical application comes from open-source packages, pulled in through manifests such as package.json, pom.xml, requirements.txt, and go.mod. Software Composition Analysis (SCA) builds a complete inventory of that third-party code and tells you what it means for your security, license, and compliance posture.

Endor Labs SCA resolves your full dependency tree — every package you declared plus everything those packages pull in — and evaluates each component for known vulnerabilities, malicious packages, license obligations, and overall package health. Findings surface in the Endor Labs platform, where security and engineering teams triage and fix them together.

💡 Direct vs. Transitive Dependencies

Direct dependencies are the packages you explicitly declare. Transitive dependencies are the packages your dependencies pull in — often the majority of the tree. You never reviewed that code, but you ship it, and a vulnerability anywhere in the tree can affect your application.

Why Open-Source Dependency Risk Matters

Open source accelerates delivery, but it also imports risk you did not author. SCA exists because that risk is real, growing, and impossible to track manually:

Risk What It Looks Like Why It Matters
Known vulnerabilities CVEs published against packages anywhere in your dependency tree Attackers scan for exploitable versions; you inherit every flaw your dependencies carry
Malicious packages Typosquatting, dependency confusion, and compromised maintainer accounts Attacks can execute at install time, before anyone reviews the code
License risk Copyleft or restricted licenses inside a proprietary product Legal and financial exposure that ships silently with transitive packages
Unhealthy packages Abandoned projects with no fixes or maintainer response Findings in these packages can linger for months with no upstream fix

The Noise Problem with Traditional SCA

Traditional SCA tools report every CVE found anywhere in the dependency tree, regardless of whether your application can actually trigger it. The result is a flood of findings: a single project can generate hundreds or thousands of alerts, and studies show that most reported CVEs are never actually invoked by the application.

The consequences are predictable. Security teams burn out triaging noise, developers stop trusting the tool, and genuinely exploitable vulnerabilities get lost in the pile. When everything is flagged “critical,” nothing is.

💡 The Endor Labs Answer: Reachability

Endor Labs’ flagship capability, function-level reachability analysis, determines whether your code actually calls a vulnerable function in a dependency — across direct and transitive packages, in 40+ languages. Only findings with a real call path into your code enter your backlog. Zebra Technologies reported cutting SCA noise by up to 97% with this approach. Module 02 covers reachability in depth.
