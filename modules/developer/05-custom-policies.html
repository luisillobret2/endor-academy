Policies: The Contract Between Security and Developers

A policy is what the platform enforces automatically, so nobody has to negotiate each finding by hand. Endor Labs applies one policy from laptop to pipeline: the rules that gate your local endorctl scan are the rules that gate the PR scan and the CI build. For developers, the deal is simple and worth internalizing — if it passes policy, it ships; if it does not, the finding tells you exactly why, and what to do about it.

💡 Three Policy Families

Endor Labs policies come in three families, and every mature rollout uses all three: finding policies decide what gets flagged and what blocks a build; exception policies define how documented risk is allowed to exist, temporarily; and remediation policies set what gets fixed, in what order, and by whom.

This module is the developer’s view of that machinery. You will not spend your days writing policies — but you will live inside them, and partners who can explain how they feel from the developer chair design rollouts that actually stick.

Finding Policies Across Every Risk Area

Finding policies cover every risk area Endor Labs analyzes, and PR scans can block pull requests when findings violate them:

Policy area What it governs Example developer effect
OSS Vulnerabilities and malicious packages in dependencies A PR introducing a reachable critical CVE fails the scan
License Licensing obligations of dependencies A new copyleft dependency is flagged for legal review
SAST Flaws in first-party code A risky pattern in your diff is commented on the PR
Secret Credentials committed to the repository Any new secret blocks the merge until it is rotated out
Container Findings in images and base images A Dockerfile change pulling a vulnerable base is gated
RSPM Registry and package repository findings Issues in your package mirrors surface on the PR that pulls from them
GitHub Action Third-party actions used in your workflows Your CI is supply chain too — a risky action is caught

The detail that keeps PRs civil: scans focus on what the pull request introduces. A PR that adds a new reachable critical finding gets blocked; pre-existing debt that has nothing to do with your change does not flood your review.

Exception Policies: Justified, Time-Boxed, Auditable

Sometimes a finding cannot be fixed right now, and pretending otherwise helps nobody. Exception policies make that conversation structured instead of furtive. A well-formed exception carries four things:

  • Justification — why the risk is acceptable for now (and it had better be more than “we are busy”)
  • Owner — a named human who is on the hook for the follow-up
  • Expiration — the date the exception resurfaces if the finding is not fixed; risk that never expires is just unmanaged debt
  • Review trail — who approved it, when, and under what conditions, visible to security and auditors

💡 A Blocked PR Is the System Working

When a policy blocks your PR, do not route around it by deleting the scanner config. File the exception instead: it takes the same two minutes, it leaves an audit trail instead of a hole, and when the expiration date arrives, the finding comes back — with its context intact — instead of being silently forgotten.

Remediation Policies and Sane Thresholds

Remediation policies set the expectations around fixing: which findings get prioritized, what the target timelines are, and who they land with. They are what turn a findings backlog into a managed queue rather than a wallpaper — and they drive the automated update PRs you met in the previous module.

The art is in the thresholds. The fastest way to kill a security program is to block everything on day one; the fastest way to grow one is to make the first gate defensible:

  1. Observe — run PR scans in report-only mode and let teams see what would have fired
  2. Warn — post findings as PR comments without blocking, so the habit forms
  3. Block narrowly — fail PRs only on reachable critical and high findings that the PR itself introduces
  4. Tighten over time — extend coverage as the team clears debt and trusts the gate

⚠️ Important for Partners

Reachability is what makes the first blocking policy defensible: the gate fires only on findings that are actually exploitable in the customer’s code, so developers cannot dismiss it as noise. When you help a customer design their first policy, start narrow and let the win rate build the mandate — policy tuning decides whether the platform is adopted or ignored.
