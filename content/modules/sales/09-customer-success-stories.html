Telling Stories That Sell

Security buyers are professionally skeptical, and for good reason: they are being asked to trust a vendor with the integrity of their software. No feature list overcomes that — but peer proof does. A concrete story about a recognizable company reducing risk and saving engineering time is the most persuasive asset you carry, and it de-risks the decision, quantifies the outcome, and builds the internal business case for you.

Master the three-beat structure and you can tell any Endor Labs story well:

  1. Situation. Who the customer is and the specific pain they walked in with — noise, remediation drag, compliance pressure.
  2. Action. What they deployed and how it fit into their existing workflow — keep this to a sentence or two.
  3. Result. The measurable outcome — a number the buyer can repeat to their own budget holder.

Two rules keep stories credible. First, tell the number as a story — “let me tell you what Zebra did” beats a statistics recital. Second, close every story with a bridge back to the prospect: “That sounds a lot like what you described in discovery — want to see what it would look like on your repos?”

Endor Labs customers include Zebra Technologies, Egnyte, Rubrik, Atlassian, Glean, Astronomer, Peloton, Netskope, Five9, MileIQ, ABC Fitness, Mysten Labs, AppLovin, Edelman Financial Engines, Snowflake, OpenAI, and Cursor — proof that the platform scales from developer tools to global enterprises. Now learn the five stories you will use most.

Zebra Technologies and Egnyte

Zebra Technologies — cutting SCA noise by 97%

Situation: A global enterprise technology company with a massive dependency surface and a security team drowning in SCA findings — every CVE in every transitive dependency landing in the backlog, and engineers burning sprints triaging vulnerabilities that were never a real risk.

Action: Deployed Endor Labs with function-level reachability analysis, so only the vulnerabilities their code actually calls — across direct and transitive dependencies — reach the backlog.

Result: A 97% reduction in SCA noise. The team fixes what is exploitable instead of working through CVEs that were never a risk. This is your anchor story for every noise-fatigued buyer.

Egnyte — 70% faster remediation and FedRAMP acceleration

Situation: A cloud content security company working to accelerate its FedRAMP journey while carrying a slow, noisy remediation process that security could not prove and engineering could not sustain.

Action: Adopted reachability analysis and AI-powered remediation — prioritizing by exploitability and driving fixes through the developer workflow.

Result: 70% faster mean time to remediation and a materially accelerated FedRAMP journey. Egnyte’s VP of Security, Dawid Balut, frames the why: reachability makes developer-level evidence credible at scale. Use this story for compliance-driven and regulated buyers.

Rubrik, Astronomer, and Glean

Rubrik — native Bazel and aggressive SLAs

Situation: A data security company with a large, complex Bazel-based build environment — where many AppSec tools struggle — and aggressive remediation SLAs leadership expected them to meet.

Action: Deployed Endor Labs with its native Bazel integration.

Result: The prior build-tooling complexity was eliminated and the team met its aggressive SLAs. This is your story for engineering-heavy buyers with build systems other tools cannot reach.

Astronomer — noise canceling for vulnerability management

Situation: The company behind Apache Airflow’s commercial platform, with an engineering-driven security culture and little patience for untrustworthy CVSS noise.

Action: Adopted reachability-based triage so findings arrive pre-filtered to real, exploitable risk.

Result: Astronomer’s CISO describes Endor Labs as “noise canceling headphones for vulnerability management.” The perfect line for developer-culture buyers — and for any prospect worried their developers won’t adopt a security tool.

Glean — reachability in an AI-native engineering org

Situation: A fast-moving AI-native company where developer velocity is the product — and where security had to hold the line without slowing anyone down.

Action: Deployed Endor Labs reachability with its AI-native integrations embedded in the developer workflow.

Result: Glean’s CISO, Sunil Agrawal, credits reachability plus AI-native integrations with keeping developers focused on real risks. This is your lead story for AI-forward accounts — pair it with AURI for the agent-governance conversation, and mention Atlassian’s Head of Product Security, Mark Turner, who has described working with Endor Labs as building security for the AI era.

Choosing the Right Story for the Room

The right story is the one that mirrors the prospect’s world: match on industry first, company size and engineering culture second, and the specific challenge third. A compliance-driven bank hears Egnyte; a dev-tools startup hears Glean or Astronomer; an enterprise with build complexity hears Rubrik; everyone with a backlog hears Zebra.

Customer Signature Result Use When
Zebra Technologies 97% reduction in SCA noise Alert fatigue, triage cost, “our backlog is unmanageable”
Egnyte 70% faster MTTR and FedRAMP acceleration Compliance pressure, regulated industry, slow remediation
Rubrik Native Bazel integration, aggressive SLAs met Complex build systems, Bazel environments, SLA commitments
Astronomer “Noise canceling headphones for vulnerability management” Developer-culture buyers, adoption concerns
Glean Developers focused on real risks in an AI-native org AI-forward accounts, velocity-first engineering teams

Handle references with discipline. Only use a customer’s name and results as approved in the Resource Library, never offer a reference call without your customer-success team’s confirmation, and let the story make one point per room — the point your discovery already surfaced.

⚠️ Protect the Reference

A reference customer is a favor to be protected, not a resource to be spent. Every misused name costs you a story — and costs Endor Labs an advocate. When in doubt about what is approved for a given account, ask before you tell.
