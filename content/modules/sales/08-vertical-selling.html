Why Vertical Tailoring Wins

Application security is a horizontal platform, but every buyer experiences it through their industry’s pressures. A financial services CISO frames findings as audit and regulatory risk; a SaaS engineering leader frames them as friction on release velocity; a public-sector buyer frames them as a compliance mandate. The platform is the same in every room — the first sentence you say should not be.

Vertical tailoring is how a commodity pitch becomes a consultative one. Open discovery with the buyer’s regulatory and business reality, not with product: “How are you handling the software supply chain requirements in your latest audit?” earns trust that “let me show you our dashboard” never will. Then translate each Endor Labs pillar into the outcome that industry actually pays for.

💡 Same Platform, Different First Sentence

Compliance buyers hear “defensible, audit-ready evidence.” Velocity buyers hear “your developers never leave their workflow.” Public-sector buyers hear “SBOM and supply chain mandates, met.” Choose the sentence before you choose the slide.

Financial Services and Regulated Enterprise

Banks, insurers, and fintechs buy application security for regulatory resilience. Examiners and enterprise customers increasingly expect SBOMs and provable open-source governance, and a finding that cannot be explained — or a critical that sat unremediated for six quarters — becomes an audit event. Reachability changes that conversation: when the backlog is only what the code actually calls, every prioritization decision is defensible with developer-level evidence.

Lead with audit-ready output: SBOM and VEX generation, policy enforcement, and a remediation trail from finding to merged fix. For firms selling into government, FedRAMP is the second hook — Egnyte used Endor Labs to accelerate its FedRAMP journey while improving mean time to remediation by 70%, and its VP of Security’s theme is exactly what a bank buyer needs to hear: reachability makes developer-level evidence credible at scale.

Discovery questions for this vertical: “What did your last audit find on open-source risk?”, “How do you produce SBOMs for commercial software today?”, and “What’s your evidence trail when a regulator asks why a critical wasn’t fixed?”

SaaS and Technology Companies

Technology companies compete on shipping speed, so the only security platform they adopt is one that never slows engineering down. This is Endor Labs’ most natural home. Lead with the developer experience: the MCP server inside Cursor, Claude Code, Codex, VS Code, Gemini CLI, Antigravity, and IntelliJ; fixes delivered as pull requests; keyless OIDC so no one manages credentials; and reachability that cuts the backlog so the noise never reaches the sprint board.

For AI-forward teams, this vertical is where the agentic story wins outright. Coding agents and MCP servers spread through engineering without central control — AURI inventories every agent, model, MCP server, and skill, enforces allow/block/ask-a-human policies, and keeps the audit trail. Glean’s CISO Sunil Agrawal describes the outcome partners should quote: reachability plus AI-native integrations keep developers focused on real risks. Astronomer’s CISO called it “noise canceling headphones for vulnerability management.” Atlassian’s Head of Product Security, Mark Turner, has described the partnership as building security for the AI era. When your prospect is a dev-tools company, mention that customers include Cursor, OpenAI, and Snowflake.

Discovery questions: “How many engineer-hours a week does triage eat?”, “Which AI coding tools are your developers actually running?”, and “What keeps malicious packages out of your CI today?” — then show Package Firewall blocking one at install time.

Healthcare, Retail, and the Public Sector

Three more verticals reward tailored framing. In each, the platform is identical — only the pressure changes.

🏥

Healthcare

Pressure: HIPAA — PHI systems can’t ship exploitable reachable code. Lead with: reachability keeping vulnerable components out of anything touching patient data, with monitoring small security teams can actually sustain.

🛒

Retail and E-commerce

Pressure: PCI DSS plus peak-season stakes — an outage in November is existential. Lead with: focusing scarce fix capacity on reachable risk in the payment path, and Package Firewall keeping typosquats and compromised packages out of the build.

🏛

Government and Public Sector

Pressure: U.S. Executive Order 14028 and the EU Cyber Resilience Act make SBOMs and supply chain security mandates, not aspirations. Lead with: SBOM/VEX generation, policy enforcement, and supply chain prevention — evidence a mandate can buy against.

For regulated U.S. cloud sellers, FedRAMP posture matters again: Egnyte’s FedRAMP acceleration demonstrates the platform holds up where the bar is highest. And for healthcare specifically, quote the noise story — Zebra Technologies’ 97% SCA noise cut is the proof that a small team can govern a large attack surface without a large headcount.
