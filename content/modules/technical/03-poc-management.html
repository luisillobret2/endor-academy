The POC Charter: Scope, Success Criteria & Stakeholders

A proof of value that ends without a decision usually began without a charter. Write one page and get it acknowledged in writing before the first scan: scope, success criteria, stakeholders, and the date of the readout.

🎯

Success criteria

Three to five measurable outcomes agreed with the economic buyer — noise-reduction percentage, reachable-finding coverage, MTTR improvement, and PR scan adoption.

📦

Scope

Three to five representative repositories spanning the customer’s real languages, one CI/CD pipeline, and one SCM. Nothing else is in scope.

👥

Stakeholders

A security champion, an engineering lead, two to three participating developers, and a named executive sponsor who owns the readout.

📅

Timeline

A typical POV runs two to four weeks. Shorter for a single team, longer only when procurement or a security review gates the start.

💡 Pro Tip

Reachability is the criterion that resonates with noise-weary teams. When a prospect says “our current tool buries us,” propose a measured comparison: total findings versus reachable findings in the same repositories. That single number carries the business case.

The 2-4 Week Plan, Week by Week

Publish the plan in the charter so the customer can hold you to it. Each week ends in a checkpoint with a written note.

Week Focus Exit criteria
Week 1 Provision the organization, connect the SCM app, configure SSO or keyless OIDC, scan the in-scope repositories All in-scope projects scanned with a working scan profile
Week 2 Triage session with the security champion; agree dismissals, exception policy templates, and reachability thresholds Baseline noise-reduction and reachable-finding numbers captured
Week 3 Developer workflow: PR scans, inline comments, upgrade impact analysis, automated pull requests, and one Endor Patch At least one merged remediation the customer can point to
Week 4 Readout: results against criteria, developer feedback, and a phased rollout plan Documented recommendation and commercial next step

⚠️ Important for Partners

Book the readout when you book the kickoff. A POV without a scheduled readout drifts into an unpaid pilot. If the customer cannot commit a date, the deal is not ready for a POV.

Evidence Collection & POV Metrics

Collect evidence continuously rather than reconstructing it at the end. Four metrics do most of the work, and each maps to a stakeholder who signs the deal.

  1. Noise reduction — total findings versus reachable findings across the same repositories, expressed as a percentage.
  2. Reachable-finding coverage — how many genuinely exploitable findings the customer had not seen, especially those hidden in transitive dependencies.
  3. MTTR — median time from finding to merged fix, measured on the POV repositories before and after the workflow change.
  4. PR scan adoption — percentage of pull requests in the POV repositories that received an Endor Labs review comment or gate.

Support the numbers with artifacts: SBOM and VEX exports for the security team, screenshots of a blocked malicious install for the platform team, and one or two direct developer quotes for the executive sponsor. Export the SBOM and VEX bundle at the end of week 2 so compliance stakeholders can review it while engineering keeps testing.

Closing the POC & Avoiding Anti-Patterns

Close in the same structure you opened: criterion, result, evidence, recommendation. Then convert the phased rollout plan into the commercial proposal.

  • Recap the criteria — one slide per criterion with the measured result.
  • Quantify the delta — for example, “1,240 findings, 210 reachable, 83% noise reduction across four repositories.”
  • Bring the developers — have a participating engineer describe the workflow in their own words.
  • Project to the estate — extrapolate from in-scope repositories to the full organization with the customer, not at them.
  • Present the rollout — team-by-team sequence, integration order, and the metrics that will confirm success.

⚠️ Anti-Patterns That Kill POVs

Boiling the ocean: onboarding hundreds of repositories so nobody can review the results. Testing unreachable CVEs: choosing flagship findings that reachability correctly deprioritizes, which undermines the value story. No baseline: running Endor Labs alone, so there is no comparison against the incumbent. Developer-free POV: testing without the people who must adopt the workflow. Endless extension: granting week five without new criteria.
