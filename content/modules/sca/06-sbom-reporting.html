What an SBOM Is and Why Regulators Ask for One

A Software Bill of Materials (SBOM) is a formal, machine-readable inventory of every component in a piece of software — your code, your direct dependencies, and the transitive dependencies behind them. If a product ships, its SBOM answers “what is inside this?” without anyone reading manifests by hand.

The demand is now regulatory and commercial:

  • EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) — places security obligations on products with digital elements sold in the EU, driving SBOM and vulnerability transparency requirements
  • US Executive Order 14028 — pushed SBOMs into federal software procurement and normalized them across enterprise buying in the US
  • Customer procurement — enterprise buyers now request SBOMs and vulnerability disclosure records as a condition of purchase

💡 The SBOM Is Now a Deliverable

For software vendors, the SBOM has moved from “nice to have” to contract terms. Teams that can generate one on demand win deals and pass reviews; teams that cannot spend weeks assembling spreadsheets. Endor Labs generates SBOMs from the same dependency data that powers findings, so the export is always current and accurate.

SBOM Formats: CycloneDX and SPDX

Endor Labs supports the two industry-standard SBOM formats:

⚙️

CycloneDX

An OWASP standard built for security use cases. Rich vulnerability and exploitability data, including support for VEX statements — the natural choice when the audience is a security team.

📜

SPDX

The Linux Foundation standard (ISO/IEC 5962). Strong on license, copyright, and provenance information — frequently expected in compliance and government contexts.

With Endor Labs you can export SBOMs in either format for your organization, applications, or projects — for example with endorctl sbom or through reports in the platform. You can also import SBOMs from third parties, so vendors’ SBOMs can be reviewed and monitored alongside your own findings rather than sitting in an email attachment.

VEX: Documenting What Is Not Exploitable

An SBOM answers “what is inside?” The next question is always “are you affected by vulnerability X?” — and answering it by re-triaging every CVE defeats the purpose. VEX (Vulnerability Exploitability eXchange) provides machine-readable statements that document, per vulnerability, whether a product is affected — including the “not affected” answers with justification.

This is where Endor Labs is different: reachability context feeds VEX. When your code has no call path to a vulnerable function, Endor Labs can issue a VEX statement saying the finding is not exploitable — backed by call-graph evidence rather than someone’s opinion. Customers, auditors, and downstream teams receive a defensible answer, not a shrug.

💡 VEX Turns Noise Reduction into a Deliverable

Reachability cuts the backlog inside your organization; VEX communicates that same reduction to everyone downstream. “We reviewed the CVE and you are not affected” becomes a structured, evidence-backed artifact that ships with the product — the difference between arguing about alerts and closing them out.

Reports and Audit Evidence

Between SBOMs and VEX, Endor Labs reporting covers the evidence a modern security program must produce:

  • SBOM export — CycloneDX or SPDX, per application or organization, on demand
  • VEX statements — exploitability answers with reachability context
  • Findings and remediation reports — what was found, what was reachable, what was fixed, and how quickly
  • License inventories — the compliance picture from Module 03, exportable for legal review
  • Audit support — evidence packages that feed FedRAMP, HIPAA, PCI, and SOC 2 audits

⚠️ Important for Partners

Compliance is a buying trigger, and reachability makes Endor Labs’ compliance story unique: competitors can export an SBOM, but few can back a “not affected” VEX statement with function-level evidence. When a customer faces EU CRA obligations, EO 14028-driven procurement, or a FedRAMP audit, lead with the evidence chain: inventory → reachability → VEX → audit package.
