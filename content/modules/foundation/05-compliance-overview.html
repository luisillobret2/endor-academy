Why Compliance Now Drives AppSec Budgets

Compliance is the fastest path from an AppSec conversation to a funded project. When a regulation sets a deadline or a customer contract requires evidence, the security team gets budget it could not win on engineering merit alone. Four drivers show up in almost every deal:

  • Regulation with deadlines — the EU Cyber Resilience Act, US Executive Order 14028, and sector rules such as FDA premarket cybersecurity guidance put software composition and vulnerability handling requirements into law with fixed dates
  • Customer and procurement requirements — enterprise buyers require SOC 2, ISO 27001, or a completed vendor security questionnaire before signing, and increasingly ask for an SBOM and a description of vulnerability handling
  • Government and public sector — FedRAMP authorization and federal software attestations require demonstrable supply chain controls and continuous vulnerability monitoring, not a one-time audit artifact
  • Insurance and board reporting — cyber insurers and boards ask for evidence of repeatable controls, and a policy-driven platform produces that evidence continuously instead of reconstructing it before an audit

💡 Compliance Is an Output of Good Security

The most persuasive framing for partners: Endor Labs does not exist to produce compliance artifacts. It produces security work, and compliance evidence falls out of the platform as SBOMs, policies, findings history, and remediation records. Customers who scan for security get audit evidence for free.

The SBOM Mandate

An SBOM (Software Bill of Materials) is the machine-readable inventory of every component in a software product: packages, versions, licenses, and identifiers. What began as a NIST and NTIA recommendation is now a procurement and regulatory requirement in multiple jurisdictions.

  • US Executive Order 14028 — directs federal agencies to require SBOMs from software suppliers, with NTIA defining the minimum data elements an SBOM must contain
  • EU Cyber Resilience Act — introduces cybersecurity obligations across the lifetime of products sold in the EU, including software composition transparency and coordinated vulnerability handling
  • FDA premarket guidance — medical device manufacturers are expected to document software components as part of cybersecurity submissions
  • Commercial contracts — enterprise and public sector buyers increasingly require an SBOM and a VEX statement as a condition of purchase

Endor Labs generates SBOMs in CycloneDX and SPDX and supports SBOM import, so an organization can consolidate third-party SBOMs into the same platform it uses for its own products. Exports are paired with VEX (Vulnerability Exploitability eXchange), which communicates whether a listed vulnerability is actually exploitable in the product rather than merely present in a component.

⚠️ Important for Partners

An SBOM alone is a list. The value in a customer conversation is the combination: SBOM for composition, reachability and VEX for exploitability, and policies for the evidence that a control was applied consistently. Sell the workflow, deliver the artifact.

Mapping Frameworks to Endor Labs Evidence

Most customers are subject to more than one framework. The same platform outputs satisfy the overlapping requirements, which is why one investment can cover several audits:

Framework or Mandate What It Requires Endor Labs Evidence
SOC 2 Documented monitoring of vulnerabilities and change management over the audit period Continuous scan history, finding policies, remediation records, and exported reports
PCI DSS Identification and remediation of vulnerabilities in bespoke and third-party software SCA with reachability, SAST on first-party code, and policy-driven remediation timelines
HIPAA Risk analysis and safeguards for systems handling protected health information Inventory of components and vulnerabilities affecting systems in scope, with exception records
FedRAMP Continuous monitoring and supply chain risk management for federal cloud services SBOM and VEX exports, container scanning, RSPM findings, and audit-ready policy evidence
ISO 27001 Managed controls for secure development and supplier relationships Policy configuration, RBAC and SSO controls, and documented handling of third-party risk
EO 14028 / EU CRA Software composition transparency and vulnerability handling across the product lifetime CycloneDX and SPDX SBOMs, VEX statements, and continuous monitoring baselines

Referenceable outcomes help here: Egnyte reduced mean time to remediate by 70% while pursuing FedRAMP, and Zebra Technologies cut vulnerability noise by 97%, which is what made continuous monitoring practical rather than aspirational.

License Compliance and Open-Source Governance

Compliance is not only about vulnerabilities. Open-source licenses carry obligations, and a copyleft dependency pulled in transitively can create disclosure requirements the engineering team never intended to accept. Endor Labs surfaces license findings alongside security findings, using the same policies and the same triage workflow.

  • License policies — define which license families are allowed, restricted, or require review, and apply them consistently across every repository
  • Exception policies — record an accepted risk with an owner and an expiry date, so an exception is a decision rather than a forgotten alert
  • Remediation policies — standardize how fixes are proposed, approved, and merged, including automated pull requests
  • RSPM — repository security posture findings, such as permissive GitHub settings, that auditors ask about under secure development controls
  • Evidence exports — SBOMs, VEX statements, and policy reports that legal, procurement, and audit teams can attach directly to a review
